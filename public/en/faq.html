Tactic Master FAQ

The questions players ask most often about Tactic Master: getting started, the rules and their edge cases, game modes, accounts and data. For the exhaustive reference, see the full rules.

Getting started

What is Tactic Master?

A turn-based football board game you play free in your browser. Pieces move one square at a time like in checkers, and you push a ball into the opposing goal. A match lasts about five minutes.

Is the game really free?

Yes. Every rule, game mode and difficulty level is available without paying. An optional shop sells pitch themes and collectible player packs: these are purely cosmetic and give no advantage in a match.

Do I need an account to play?

No. Local modes — solo against the computer, two players on one device, daily puzzle, penalty shoot-out — start without signing up. An account is only needed for online multiplayer, the shop, squad management and friends.

Do I need to install anything?

No. The game opens in your browser, on desktop and mobile alike. You can add it to your home screen on iOS and Android — it is a web app (PWA), not an app-store download.

Can I play offline?

Yes for local modes, once the page has loaded or been installed as a PWA. Online multiplayer naturally requires a connection.

Is the game available in English?

Yes. The interface is fully translated; this page and the full rules are in English. Strategy articles are currently written in French only.

What age is it suitable for?

The Discovery rule tier works from around seven or eight: move a piece, push the ball, score, with no interception rules at all. The Classic and Expert tiers add the tactical depth adults look for.

Rules

How do I score?

By bringing the ball to rest on any of the three central squares of the opponent's back row. There is no other condition — no minimum distance, no required number of passes.

Can I capture enemy pieces?

Never. This is the fundamental difference from checkers. No piece leaves the pitch during the whole match: enemy pieces are worked around, not removed.

Can the ball travel through a piece?

No. A pass stops at the first piece it meets, friendly or not. Two exceptions: the Power Shot, which goes through the first piece, and a shot fired from the penalty spot, which pierces one outfield defender but never a goalkeeper.

Do I have to push the ball as far as possible?

No, and this is the most common beginner mistake. Every free square along the path is a valid destination — you choose where the ball stops. A one-square push is legal, and often better, because it keeps the ball under your control.

Can a piece already touching the ball push it without moving first?

Yes. Select it, then click the destination square: the pass happens directly. A piece that reaches the ball after a move can also follow up with a pass in the same turn.

Can I move a piece onto the ball's square?

No. The ball's square counts as occupied: you do not step on it and you never carry the ball. You move beside it in order to push it.

What is defensive cover?

A square is covered by a team when one of its outfield pieces stands directly above, below, left or right of it. An opposing pass can neither stop on nor travel through a covered square. Cover is strictly orthogonal, so diagonals stay open.

Does the goalkeeper cover the squares around it?

No, and that is deliberate. The keeper is the only piece that covers nothing. If it projected a cover zone, the goal would be impossible to breach. It defends by physically occupying one square of the goal.

Can the goalkeeper leave its goal?

No. It slides sideways across the three squares of its goal line and never leaves the back row.

Is there an offside rule?

No. No offside, no fouls, no cards, no corners, no throw-ins. The ball never leaves the pitch — it stops against the edge.

What happens after a goal?

The ball returns to the centre and every piece goes back to its starting square. The team that conceded restarts play.

How many goals win a match?

Three in the standard setup. Time-limited formats end after a fixed number of turns, and the score then decides the winner.

What happens if the scores are level?

In a time-limited format, a level score at the end of the turns is a draw, settled by a penalty shoot-out — a separate arcade mode with aiming and a power gauge.

What if nobody touches the ball any more?

After eight consecutive turns without a single pass — four per side — the ball is automatically returned to the centre for a neutral restart, if the centre square is free. The pieces are not moved.

What is the one-two?

If the ball comes to rest orthogonally next to one of your outfield pieces at the end of your pass, your team immediately plays a bonus move. One move only, never a second pass, and it never stacks.

What is the penalty spot?

The central square two rows from the opposing goal. A shot from there ignores cover and pierces one outfield defender, but never the goalkeeper. This rule is only active at the Expert tier.

Modes and difficulty

Which game modes are available?

Solo against the computer at three levels, two players on one device, online multiplayer via a game code, the daily puzzle and the penalty shoot-out.

How do the three computer levels differ?

Easy plays largely at random and never pushes the ball more than two squares, so it does not punish a beginner's mistake. Medium takes any immediate scoring chance and favours moves that bring the ball closer to your goal. Hard additionally anticipates your best reply before choosing its move, and uses its powers.

What is the difference between Discovery, Classic and Expert?

Discovery disables all interception: passes are only blocked by pieces themselves. Classic, the default, adds defensive cover and the one-two. Expert also enables crosses from the wings and the penalty spot, on an eight-piece formation.

Can I customise the rules?

Yes. Advanced options let you switch cover, the one-two, crosses and the penalty spot on or off individually, on top of the chosen tier.

How do I play with a friend?

On the same device, each player takes their turn on the same screen. Remotely, one player creates an online game and shares the code.

What is the daily puzzle?

A preset position to solve within a given number of moves, the same for everyone on a given day. Puzzles use the Discovery tier, which keeps them unambiguous and guarantees a solution exists.

Account, shop and support

What can I buy in the shop?

Pitch themes and collectible player packs, strictly cosmetic. No purchase changes the rules or gives any in-match advantage.

How do I delete my account and my data?

From your account, "Export my data" and "Delete my account" let you respectively obtain a copy of your data and erase everything. Details are in the privacy policy.

Does the game show ads?

No advertising script is loaded until you have explicitly consented, and consent can be withdrawn at any time from "Manage my data preferences".

Which browsers are supported?

Recent versions of Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari, on desktop and mobile. The game uses no plugins.

The page will not update, or something looks stuck.

The game keeps a local copy of its files so it can work offline, which can delay a new version. Force-reload the page (Ctrl+F5, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac).

I found a bug, or I have a suggestion.

Both are welcome — the contact page explains how to report them, by email or directly on the project's public repository.

Still stuck?

Write to us — the contact page lists the two ways to reach the team. Recurring questions eventually make it onto this page.
